## Findings in priority order

### F1 · High · Proven state-writer behavior, scope decision on generic postings

**Impact and requirement.** A caller able to construct a balanced ordinary journal can directly debit a household BANK without a qualified member route or original. This permits an outflow to enter balances with only the transaction ID. Executable §§5 and 16 require explicit funding and atomic state, and PFA-DET §5.11 S1/S4 and [#276](https://github.com/bensmullen/personal-finance-app/issues/276) require source-qualified domain postings to use shared authority. Ordinary transfers and legacy generic journals also use the state writer. Scope of the defect is the low-level direct path for a journal shaped like a qualified domain posting. External reachability and any requirement to authenticate all generic transfers were outside the review.

**Expected versus actual.** A domain BANK posting should fail without qualification. `src/state/index.ts:2814–2833` enforces the approval token only when a registration is supplied. With none, lines 2835–2841 change cash and lines 2865–2891 skip archive admission but commit the validated candidate. `test/d2/integrated/householdBankAuthority/authority.test.ts:75–85` explicitly performs this unregistered post and then checks that backfill fails. Its title describes a bypass rejection, while the assertion rejects the supplied forged registration. State retains the identity and balance from the unregistered post.

**Independent oracle, unexecuted.** Against the test's synthetic shared BANK, post a balanced $1.00 expense debit and BANK credit through `applyAccountingTransactionAtomically` without registration. For a source-qualified expense, expect identical cash, identities and archive on rejection. The writer path predicts a $1.00 cash decrease and one transaction ID with no proof. Run the matching qualified case, and retain a legitimate owned transfer and legacy Person BANK journal as controls. Check both no-election and payroll/election callers when #277 merges. #284/#289 add token gates and proof revalidation, and #288 qualifies the explicit expense route. Their presence does not change the observed registration-free branch. #149 owns the distinction between admissible generic operations and source-qualified domain journal authority.

### F2 · High · Credible hypothesis requiring reproduction · Unfunded expense provenance across a checkpoint

**Impact and requirement.** An unpaid sourced payable may carry forward into net worth after the original publisher changes or disappears. Executable §§16.1–16.2 require reproducible results and typed source provenance, and PFA-DET §5.11 S4/S7 requires source-linked affected results. An unfunded claim is expected to remain a legitimate liability with no BANK debit or settlement identity.

**Expected versus actual.** `src/simulation/verticalSlice2.ts:739–779` qualifies both sources before recognition and rereads them on the unfunded branch. The recognition stores generated provenance and the obligation stores the recognition ID and traces. Only a funded settlement produces the typed BANK proof. `src/simulation/r3/replayArtifact.ts:250–272` requalifies archived source histories, and `src/state/index.ts:2494–2526` enumerates source-bearing posting proofs and settlement receipts. `src/simulation/householdExecution.ts:1399–1413` can resume after recognition. A checkpoint containing the unpaid claim alone appears to have no event-scoped economic-original record for the cold importer to requalify. Replaying the recognition period itself or a later funded event can independently hit the source, so the hypothesis is restricted to a post-checkpoint window without either read.

**Independent oracle, unexecuted.** Put one $25.37 qualified expense in month 1 with $10.00 in BANK, end that schedule, and export after 13 months. The month-12 checkpoint should preserve $10.00 BANK and the $25.37 payable. In a fresh process with the publisher unavailable, select month 13. Compare with month 1 and a funded counterpart. If Phase 2 confirms historical requalification applies to this original, expect rejection or an explicitly source-incomplete result for the affected payable, preserving cash and all historical IDs. #288 covers precommit reread and funded cold-import evidence (`expenses.test.ts:51,171,191`); absence of a settlement proof on the unpaid branch is intentional. The question concerns durable recognition provenance. Review jointly with #149 and dependent premium/Medicare owners before interpreting the result.

### F3 · Medium · Spec ambiguity · Incomplete replacement and the last good result

**Impact and requirement.** A replacement that stops early can displace a complete forecast and its retained evidence. `src/simulation/AGENTS.md:15–16` protects the last successful result after incomplete execution. The R2 milestone requires retention of the last valid result during replacement, and PFA-DET-014/§5.11 S7 require truthful incomplete coverage. A fully executed horizon with a scoped tax gap can provide valid before-tax results, as `test/d1IntegratedCloseout.test.ts:115–120` demonstrates.

**Expected versus actual.** If a stopped period makes the replacement unsuccessful for retention, preserve the earlier complete result as stale while exposing the replacement diagnostic. `src/application/interactiveForecast.ts:146–175,215–218` accepts a contiguous incomplete prefix, uses the same cacheability predicate for all incomplete results, and updates `lastGoodResult`. `ui/forecast/controller.ts:296–307,358–375` releases the prior evidence owner on replacement and admits any transport-valid incomplete response. Existing `test/r2InteractiveExecution.test.ts:187,246,260` exercises intentionally admitted incomplete results. The unresolved contract is whether a hard-stopped prefix qualifies for the same retention behavior as a full horizon with a scoped gap.

**Independent oracle, unexecuted.** Start with a complete two-month result, submit a changed case that commits month 1 then fails with a typed validation error in month 2, and deliver the incomplete prefix. Compare the retained result, available evidence and stale status with a full-horizon tax-incomplete replacement and a superseded worker response. Route lifecycle semantics to #120 and frontier meaning to #149.

### F4 · Medium · Missing/weak test · Historical v1/v2 bytes

**Impact and requirement.** A portable archive from a previous release can become unreadable without the compatibility test detecting it. Executable §16.3 and ADR-011 establish explicit compatibility for personal model versions. For execution archives, the decoder's advertised v1/v2 support is the specific compatibility claim under review. `src/simulation/r3/replayArtifact.ts:221–270` admits v1/v2 with opening replay and v3 with checkpoint deltas. `test/portableHouseholdReplayCheckpoints.test.ts:133–139` relabels the current v3 encoding and omits integrity. It exercises dispatch and opening replay against current wire content. It cannot detect loss of compatibility with genuinely older value encodings or participant configuration.

**Expected versus actual.** Assert replay against independently preserved historical synthetic bytes with exact balances, IDs and source availability behavior. Existing tests cover corrupt and unknown versions, checkpoint mismatches and fresh current module/provider boundaries, which constrain distinct failures. **Oracle, unexecuted:** retain one real synthetic export per supported historical version, compare first and late period journals/cents with release-specific expectations, and confirm no portable checkpoint is advertised for opening-only versions. Archive reviewers under #149 should confirm supported historical versions and producer fixtures.

### F5 · Medium · Credible hypothesis requiring reproduction · Cross-domain component boundary

**Impact and requirement.** Wrong same-instant ordering can select which obligations receive cash or reject a valid plan. Executable §3.4.2 requires economic independence or declared precedence. `src/simulation/householdExecution.ts:279–289,661–741` groups only cross-domain shared consumed cash, restricts comparison edges to group endpoints, and runs other same-instant operations afterward in lexical order for comparison. Final execution uses full resolved dependencies at lines 818–834. `intraperiodScheduler.ts:116–130` validates the declared graph and `r3/reachableStates.ts:39–67` enumerates the supplied graph. These checks do not establish that terminal comparisons respect dependencies between a group and its remainder, or that every extension domain declares its own same-domain ordering.

**Expected versus actual.** Compare outcomes of full dependency-valid orders using opening cash, claims, diagnostics and resource effects. The component comparison can exercise a different prefix than actual execution. **Oracle, unexecuted:** synthetic extension A requires a same-instant setup operation X, A competes with B for one named $1.00 account, and X is outside the cash contention component. X establishes a prerequisite in operation runtime, with A rejecting if it runs first. Full valid orders always put X before A. Compare the component decision with hand-enumerated orders and vary input order/IDs; add two same-domain competing operations as a control. Existing bounded exhaustive checks and hard-failure preservation cover the supplied component graph. Analytical proofs apply only to the two specifically shaped cases in `r3/commutativity.ts:4–28`. #149 should retest with actual D2 participant descriptor graphs before making a product reachability claim.

## Growth, privacy and false-positive controls

- One proof per distinct posting is expected. The inspected 1/12/24 tests bound proof sizes, provider reads and absence of inventory markers for those fixtures. A repeated large immutable catalog can still produce a linear slope in period count. Measure UTF-8 bytes, provider calls, unique graph definitions, changed rows and peak heap at 1/12/24 months with two independent catalog sizes, holding occurrence count fixed when increasing catalog size. Expect one catalog definition or scoped pin, with only occurrence IDs, signed journals, typed references and bounded overhead growing with periods. At 12 months there is no terminal checkpoint; at 24 there is one prior annual checkpoint. Measure cold restore separately from warm replay and from any JSON serialization of per-row addresses. The existing 24/48 test deliberately constructs repeated JSON to quantify amplification. It does not establish the production worker transfer repeats it.
- The graph codec shares nested object definitions by identity and encoded content (`replayArtifact.ts:25–52`) and state uses sparse indexed changes. Diagnostic merging and checkpoint comparison explicitly avoid expanding the same catalog at every warning. Distinct cumulative arrays, opaque strings and participant-specific snapshots still deserve measurements. `reachableStates.ts:44–70` can visit many distinct subsets/states when exact prefix merging rarely applies. This is a bounded-fixture scaling risk under PFA-PERF-018/021; no elapsed-time conclusion follows.
- Portable replay encodes configuration, opening state, period summaries and checkpoint changes. Exact decimal strings are still sensitive financial data. Inspect worker payloads, storage, export and diagnostic logging for household IDs and balances using synthetic markers only. `replayArtifact.ts:207–215` explicitly treats integrity as change detection and distinguishes a private retained basis from cold transport. The checksum is not a privacy control or evidence that providers were authenticated. ADR-017 documents plaintext personal model export; this audit did not inspect device storage, telemetry or user data.
- Checked the pinned merged history for #284 (original BANK route and generic proof), #288 (VS2 qualified expenses), #289 (native receipts), #290 (card/after-tax sources) and #291 (retirement/standalone Medicare). A late-known publication, ordinary Person-owned BANK journal, pure transfer, unavailable tax law and an honestly unpaid claim each have different semantics from a fabricated payment or duplicate recognition. No finding treats an unpaid obligation, supported-scope incompleteness, a non-authenticating change fingerprint, or sparse checkpoint replay as inherently defective.

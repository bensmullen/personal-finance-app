## 1. Architecture-wide resolved invariants (existing authority, not new PM choices)

1. Retain canonical financial vocabulary and executable-semantics authority for identity, money, date/period, state/flow/recognition, obligation, settlement and balanced postings. Use signed integer cents, exact or rational arithmetic and explicit rounding conventions. Never use independent D2 ledger/tax engines.
2. Each economic operation has a stable event ID, effective civil date, actor/owner, referenced accounts/plan/beneficiary, authorization/evidence source and rule-version reference. Candidate computation is pure; no authoring or simulator module mutates authoritative state.
3. Shared coordinator owns ordered, atomic validation → funding/reservation → effects → tax-fact emission → settlement → committed ledger/state. Reject an unfunded/illegal operation in its entirety; do not consume limit headroom, basis, cash or entitlement on failure. Prevent double counting of wrapper and holdings, gross income versus principal movements, gross premium versus paid employee share, and withholding versus tax expense.
4. Distinguish (a) scenario/forecast assumptions from (b) sourced historical facts and (c) effective-dated law/plan terms. Store historical snapshots, provenance and resolved rule-set versions. A future projection cannot silently assert current law persists; use an explicit projection policy and disclose material uncertainty/incompleteness.
5. Unsupported legal/plan-specific branches are **typed diagnostics with affected-result incompleteness**, not zeros, fallback generic income/expense, permissive transfers, invented rates or invented statutory eligibility. Full D2 completion still requires every ordinary family in DET-016 through DET-022.

## 3. Proposed exact economic contracts

### A1 — Social Security and pensions [DET-016; TAX-015]

**Social Security.** Own a benefit entitlement per recipient tied to the applicable worker record (including spouse/deceased worker) and an explicit benefit kind. Inputs: recipient/worker IDs; birth/death/marriage facts used; PIA/FRA or quoted estimate with quote date, dollar basis, claiming-age basis and assumed COLA; actual claiming and commencement months; observed work/earnings when earnings test applies; spouse/worker eligibility; versioned SSA adjustments and future-COLA policy. A quoted **at-claim-age** benefit is never reduced again for age. A quoted **at-FRA** benefit requires an explicit, law-versioned age adjustment. Retirement and spousal awards are coordinated, not added; own retirement is paid first with an eligible spousal excess when applicable. Survivor eligibility and election can replace or coordinate with own benefit after worker death; not an unconditional second check. Model benefit reduction/suspension (including work-test) separately from credited withholding and Medicare withholding. Create gross recognized Social Security income, actual gross payable, optional tax withholding credit, benefit-funded Medicare premium obligation and net cash settlement. TAX-015 computes taxable benefit using combined household provisional-income logic: never mark 85% of gross automatically taxable. Unsupported SSA family maximum, disability, child-in-care, GPO/WEP historical applicability and complex divorced-claim scenarios require scoped legal rules or explicit diagnostic.

**Pension.** Participant, provider, election/annuity form, start/end, benefit frequency, selected survivor percentage/continuation, named joint annuitant, mortality scenario and COLA method. Admit provider-quoted gross periodic amount or a formula only if all service, compensation, accrual, early-retirement and option inputs are authoritative; never synthesize a plan formula. Participant death stops or transitions per contracted form. Gross pension income and withholding are distinct; any previously taxed employee contribution is tracked as investment in contract with applicable IRS Simplified Method / General Rule and exhaustion of basis. Quotes and survivor elections must specify whether amount is pre-/post-election. Accept tests for spouse death, earlier/later commencement, COLA changes, tax-free basis depletion and no double claim.

### A2 — expanded plans [DET-017; INV-025; TAX-015]

Represent plan family plus subtype (governmental-457 explicitly; federal TSP explicitly), participant, sponsoring employer/government agency, account tax bucket (Traditional/Roth/nondeductible after-tax only when allowed), eligible payroll/employer sources, plan features, eligibility and effective date. Store **per-person per-tax-year statutory usage by legal scope**, separate from employer-plan-specific annual additions, compensation base and employer match; include actual year and rule snapshot. Payroll salary deferrals reduce take-home as appropriate; employer-funded contributions increase owned plan assets without reducing household cash. Traditional deferral changes wages/tax facts; Roth employee deferrals use after-tax wage character. SEP ordinary contribution is employer-funded only (not salary reduction); self-employment contributions require correct self-employed compensation calculation. SIMPLE employee deferrals and obligatory employer match/nonelective are distinct; track two-year SIMPLE rollover restrictions. A governmental 457(b) has independent deferral capacity where applicable; don't absorb it into 401(k)/403(b) combined limit, and preserve its withdrawal/rollover exception differences. 403(b) special 15-year and 457 special final-three-year catch-up remain diagnostics absent requisite service/history; ordinary age catch-up supported with plan eligibility. TSP has its own plan capability descriptor (do not infer mega-backdoor from account name). Special plan-level after-tax/rollover acceptance must be explicit, not default true.

2026 illustration **only**, never constants in model: ordinary 401(k)/403(b)/TSP elective deferral $24,500, regular catch-up $8,000, ages 60–63 catch-up $11,250, defined-contribution annual additions generally $72,000; ordinary SIMPLE deferral $17,000 with employer-size/program variants; IRA $7,500, plus $1,100 catch-up; SEP employer max generally $72,000 subject to compensation rules. Laws, effective year, plan documents, employee ages, source type and sharing scope govern actual limits. Acceptance: simultaneous 401(k)+403(b) appropriately share deferral space; governmental 457 has correctly independent bucket; failed payroll funding commits no contribution or capacity; employer contribution is not household expense; TSP / SEP / SIMPLE cannot silently inherit 401(k) eligibility.

### A3 — IRA and mega-backdoor [DET-018; INV-026; TAX-015]

Traditional IRA→Roth conversion is asset movement plus tax recognition, **not** wages, spending, Roth regular contribution or additional annual-deferral usage. On a calendar-tax-year/person basis, IRA basis allocation uses Form 8606 facts aggregating **Traditional, SEP and SIMPLE IRAs**, December-31 aggregate values and year distributions/conversions (including applicable outstanding rollovers); retain year-end reconciliation, remaining basis and special death-year exceptions. This requires an end-of-year tax fact resolution pass (or equivalent deterministic lookahead); month-level cash movements cannot pretend final pro-rata tax amount is known before future year-end IRA facts. Respect basis recovered across all that year's distributions, not each conversion independently. Roth IRA keeps separate regular contribution, conversion/rollover vintage + taxable/nontaxable amount, and earnings ordering/qualification clocks.

A backdoor flow is (1) funded nondeductible Traditional IRA **regular contribution** consuming ordinary IRA compensation/annual-limit eligibility, then (2) conversion with aggregate basis and tax fact, possibly in different tax years. Direct Roth contribution income limits are not used as a conversion eligibility shortcut. A mega flow is (1) funded employee **after-tax, non-Roth** workplace contribution consuming eligible §415 annual additions and plan headroom, followed by either (2a) accepted direct in-plan Roth conversion or (2b) eligible distribution and source-allocated Roth IRA rollover; the conversion/rollover does **not** consume IRA regular-contribution capacity. A partial after-tax plan distribution normally carries proportional pretax earnings; supported Notice 2014-54 split destinations must respect one-distribution allocation. Existing D1 401(k) in-plan conversion is reused, not duplicated. Acceptance: other SEP IRA balances materially change conversion taxable percentage; Roth conversion preserves NW before recognized tax; after-tax basis moves exactly once; plan disallowance stops mega path without losing contribution; signed/cash settlement and tax liability separate.

### A4 — ordinary distributions and RMD [DET-004/017; TAX-015; issue #103]

A distribution request specifies owner/account/eligible holding, requested gross amount or units, calendar event/recurrence, actual liquid/cash availability or explicit sale, funding bank account, withholding instruction, source/tax-bucket character and rule snapshot. A security sale is a separate intra-wrapper liquidation; a gross withdrawal reduces owned retirement resources and increases external bank cash net of withholding, with withholding a tax credit (not a consumption event). Tax engine handles Traditional pre-tax/basis, Roth IRA ordering/5-year qualification, designated Roth plan rules, age/plan exemptions and possible early distribution penalties. Direct rollovers and conversions are different operation kinds; no ordinary RMD can be rolled over.

Model RMD as an **annual requirement/obligation** based on owner DOB cohort, prior-year eligible account balance(s), correct IRS life-table denominator, beneficiary/spouse table and retirement/still-working exceptions when applicable. Track eligible withdrawals actually satisfied and shortfall as separate state, not an automatic invisible transfer. Default UI policy proposed: explicitly schedule compliant distribution into named bank account where fully supported; require a funding/sale policy; otherwise show a blocker and RMD shortfall, never spend ghost liquidity. First-year April-1 timing and possible two RMDs next year are material. SECURE 2.0 cohort age 73/75 rules are date-of-birth dependent; owner-lifetime Roth IRA and designated Roth accounts generally have no RMD. Inherited IRA, QCD and complex exceptions require separate admission. Acceptance: age cohort transition, prior-Dec-31 snapshot, two-RMD-year, no rollover, no early penalty misclassification, no tax/asset double-count, insufficient liquidity rollback. Consolidated #146 now owns the **full unimplemented #103 scope** under approved phase-specific V3 authorizations; #103 remains a closed provenance link, not completed functionality.

### B1 — consumer debt [DET-019]

Shared debt profile inputs: instrument/account/borrower/lender, signed contractual balance, loan start/as-of, rate/effective day-count, compounding/accrual schedule, due dates, minimum/scheduled payment, autopay bank source, draw authority, extra principal, grace/capitalization eligibility and payoff/closing terms. Fixed-rate amortizing **auto and ordinary student** loans reuse P22/VS4 where terms are complete: accrued interest first, scheduled required funding first, extra capped to principal, no hidden debt draw; ordinary student profile is standard repayment, not IDR/deferment. Credit cards use a **statement cycle** with card purchases/refunds/payment transactions and a stated APR/daily accrual convention; interest posts once, then required minimum determined by **explicit issuer formula/statement quote** (e.g. documented fixed floor plus interest plus portion of principal), not one universal statutory minimum. HELOC has distinct draw and repayment periods, authorized line/draw limits, rate index as-of/reset date, spread/caps/floors, interest convention, interest-only versus amortization schedule, payment/reset and final balloon/payoff policy. If rate index or minimum rule is missing, prospective after-tax/cash results are incomplete, not frozen at last rate. Funding a debt draw adds equal cash and liability, while principal repayment reduces both; interest is expense. Acceptance: partial payoff; zero interest; negative amortization only if explicitly permitted; draw over-limit; missing reset; statement grace behavior; blocked funding; payoff/extra prioritization.

### B2 — TIPS and Series EE [DET-021; INV-027; TAX-015]

TIPS is a **dated CPI-indexed security**, not generic floating coupon bond. Inputs: CUSIP/issue, original principal, coupon, dated CPI-U reference/index ratios (or published Treasury daily ratios), rate/coupon dates, settlement lots/purchase price/accrued-interest, wrapper type, maturity and adjusted basis. Compute principal adjustment and coupon on adjusted principal using published Treasury CPI lag/interpolation/rounding as specified per issue; at maturity pay **greater of original par and inflation-adjusted principal**, with proper accounting of tax basis and any floor gain. In taxable wrappers, coupon is federal interest and positive inflation accretion is generally federally taxable noncash OID with matching basis adjustment; deflation, indexed gain and holding/disposal require appropriate source-specific rules. Missing index observations are unsupported forecast segments, not zero inflation. Unsupported TIPS secondary-market cases are diagnosed unless adequate lot/price/tax facts exist.

Series EE requires issue month/vintage, purchase price, face/original maturity, vintage-specific fixed/variable rules, interest election, redemption date and tax jurisdiction. For modern EE, interest accrues monthly, compounds semiannually, has 20-year doubling guarantee (issue-vintage applicability), stops earning at 30 years; no cash redemption before 12 months, 3-month interest forfeiture before 5 years. Redemption moves bond asset value to bank cash exactly once; federal interest, state/local exemption and election to recognize annually versus at redemption have explicit tax basis/previously recognized interest state. A 2026 new-issue rate is **issue-specific**, not a constant for existing bonds or future vintages. Acceptance: index reduction/maturity floor; taxable accretion without bank cash; guaranteed EE year-20 adjustment; year-4 penalty; redemption before 1 year rejected; tax election with no duplicate recognition.

### B3 — 529 education and rollovers [DET-022; INV-028; TAX-015]

529 is owner-controlled, beneficiary-linked invested economic holdings with tracked contributions/basis and earnings; account holdings count once in household net worth (not a second wrapper asset). Transactions: externally funded contributions, earnings/valuation, qualified distributions tied to supported dated **adjusted** education expenses, nonqualified distributions with pro-rata basis/earnings and tax/additional-tax facts, plan-to-plan eligible rollovers and legal beneficiary changes. Prevent double use of one tuition/loan expense against multiple benefits and 529 withdrawals. A state jurisdiction rule is required to claim deductions/credits, recapture or tax savings; otherwise show federal result and state incompleteness, not zero state tax.

529→Roth IRA is a **direct trustee transfer** only to beneficiary's Roth IRA, with plan account open >=15 years, eligible funds excluding contributions/earnings attributable to the last 5 years, $35,000 lifetime beneficiary rollover cap, remaining tax-year Roth IRA contribution limit/compensation capacity and ownership eligibility. Model dated contribution lots and plan-account age; do not assume an ordinary direct Roth contribution or an IRA conversion. Legal interpretation of whether beneficiary changes restart the 15-year clock and how some intermediary plan transfers affect aging must be explicitly sourced/capability-gated where unsettled. Acceptance: 14-year rejection, 5-year exclusion, recipient mismatch, combined annual capacity, lifetime exhausted, ineligible expense, proportional nonqualified earnings, no contribution double-count, state-unknown diagnostic.

### C1/C2 — health premiums and Medicare [DET-020; INS-013; TAX-015]

Health coverage has enrolled members, payer/employer, coverage start/end, gross monthly premium, employer share and household share, direct after-tax bank settlement versus §125 qualified pretax payroll deduction, and plan/jurisdiction tax treatment. The **gross bill is not also an expense when employer subsidy + employee share are accounted**: recognize employer subsidy/benefit only under its actual wage/tax rules and deduct employee-paid cash/premium once. A family losing coverage or switching jobs terminates old schedule; plan overlap requires explicit coordination, not auto-deduction twice. Avoid assuming tax deductibility for after-tax premiums without itemization/self-employment facts.

Medicare is per member: Part A premium status, Part B election, Part D plan, Medicare Advantage/Medigap/supplement as elected, Medicare effective dates, separate plan premium and **IRMAA** charge. Do not charge incompatible Part C + Medigap combinations as normal-supported without validation. Compute yearly surcharge via IRMAA income/filer lookup using the authoritative prior tax-year MAGI and current year's CMS brackets; normal lookback is 2 tax years (2026 generally reads 2024). Life-changing-event appeals/alternate determinations are sourced overrides, not silent heuristic changes. An SSA-benefit premium deduction reduces benefit cash *after gross benefit and premium liability recognition*, while direct bank-paid premium settles the same obligation via bank; never both. No Social Security yet is not equivalent to Medicare ineligible. 2026 example: standard monthly Part B **$202.90** with CMS income-tier surcharges; 2027+ cannot inherit this as verified law. Acceptance: two covered members; mid-year enrollment; employer/individual overlap; partial-year IRMAA tier; deceased participant stop; benefit-deducted versus direct-paid parity and single premium cost.

### C3 — health FSA [DET-020; TAX-015]

Model employer plan **plan-year** with per-member election, pre-tax wage reduction source, employer contributions if any, plan-year cash/elected total versus **uniformly available reimbursement cap** (benefits are not limited to paycheck-funded-to-date contributions), substantiated qualified incurred expenses and receipts/reimbursements, designated bank destination and selected carryover/grace/lapse term. Do not model available reimbursement as ordinary liquid investment/cash or double-add to net worth: it is a conditional employer-plan entitlement. Contribution reduces employee taxable wages and take-home per legally applicable income/payroll bases; a reimbursed qualified medical claim creates a payment/offset/settlement, **not a second wage income**. Distinguish expense incurred/paid and reimbursement so household net worth changes only via actual wages, cash, consumption and recoverable claim facts. Store forfeited unused eligibility at year end; carryover/grace only if plan offers it and compliant rule version permits it. 2026 plan-year employee salary reduction ceiling $3,400; optional max carryover $680, not automatic. Dependent-care FSA and HSA are different families. Acceptance: January reimbursement exceeding YTD withheld but within election; insufficient plan eligible entitlement; same receipt can't reimburse twice; no invented household FSA asset; carryover versus grace mutually valid; reimbursement fails without expense proof; no double salary-tax reduction.

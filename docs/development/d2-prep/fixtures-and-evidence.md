# Fixture contract and evidence boundaries

`test/fixtures/d2/catalog.json` contains 55 synthetic cases with stable, case-local symbolic identities. `schema.ts` supplies the TypeScript type and strict runtime schema; `inputContracts.ts` independently enumerates the required amounts, terms and roles for each of the 55 family/variant pairs. This private test vocabulary is deliberately not a canonical model or a future compiler interface. Each case identifies its D2 lane, family/variant, normative requirements/anchors, half-open period, source/beneficiary/plan facts, uncertainty and capability boundary.

All monetary quantities are signed integer **USD cents represented as strings**. Quoted amounts are synthetic contract facts, not estimates of tax law or benefit eligibility. Rate conventions are explicit where the existing P22 profile is used; unknown rate/index/legal conventions remain missing inputs. Money calculations in the test-only reconciliation use `bigint`. Nothing uses floating money, randomness, clocks, network requests, real household data or mutable global state.

`decision_required` means a future financial/product contract must be resolved before admitting execution. `not_yet_executable` means the bounded stated behavior already has reusable authority, but this fixture does not prove a supported D2 runtime path. Neither status claims financial-law completeness. Every case has nonempty `unknownOutputs`; absence of an `effects` object means those monetary outputs are unknown, never zero.

Where present, `effects` is a **conditional projection of explicitly supplied economic facts**. Opening accounts, transaction legs, ending balances, cash delta, recognition and net-worth delta are independently stated literals. They are not calculated by a proposed D2 helper. For example:

- A quoted 1,000.00 benefit with an authored 100.00 withholding reconciles 900.00 cash plus a 100.00 tax-credit asset and 1,000.00 gross recognition. Tax expense/liability and eligibility remain unknown; the withholding is not spending.
- A 500.00 same-owner IRA conversion movement changes neither net worth nor consumption. Aggregate IRA taxable amount/basis is deliberately absent. The separate existing D1 same-plan 401(k) conversion case can state 100.00 recovered basis and 400.00 taxable conversion under executable §21; that is not an IRA aggregate/pro-rata rule or a complete mega-backdoor chain.
- P22 zero-rate service and the exact one-remaining-month final payment reconcile principal and interest without implementing a second amortization formula. Failed all-or-nothing required funding commits nothing, including voluntary extra principal.
- A quoted TIPS coupon or EE redemption reconciles only the quoted cash/recognition. No inflation index, EE guarantee or tax election is invented.
- A 529 contribution or eligible same-owner plan transfer preserves consolidated net worth. Qualification, earnings allocation, penalties, recapture and 529-to-Roth statutory values remain unresolved.
- An authored after-tax health/Medicare premium reduces cash and net worth once. Quoted premium arithmetic does not establish Medicare eligibility/IRMAA. FSA reimbursement availability/ownership is unresolved, so no synthetic asset/expense amount is asserted.

Ledger accounts are economic resources or recognition accounts, not wrapper totals. Position, cash and withholding-credit assets are counted once; liabilities subtract from net worth. The test ledger contains no separate wrapper asset that could duplicate its holdings. It excludes unstated tax expense/settlement, fees, valuation changes and other future events as its condition explicitly describes. This gross projection is not an after-tax forecast. Empty transactions represent only explicitly bounded failed/excluded operations; they are not a missing-rule fallback.

`test/d2/verifyCatalog.ts` is a test-only consistency checker, not an authoritative executor. It verifies uniqueness and account-owner references, valid periods, per-transaction balance in the declared currency, exact literal ending balances, cash/recognition/net-worth reconciliation and nonnegative economic balances. It also invokes the family/role contract and the conditional economic assertions described below. `catalog.test.ts` pins ordinary family/variant coverage, real normative anchors, lane ownership, #103 reuse, capability boundaries and selected independently stated examples. Negative cases detect invalid money/dates, missing uncertainty, duplicate IDs, invalid references, unbalanced legs, changed expected balances/NW, negative balances and postings at the exclusive end.

Identity declarations now distinguish people, employers, plans and symbolic accounts. Required role references resolve to those kinds; economic accounts have explicit owners and family/operation-specific account types (for example Traditional versus Roth IRA, payroll versus bank cash, or the named retirement-plan family). Retirement/education account roles identify their related plan where appropriate. Plans identify their family and, where represented, employer sponsor. The mega-backdoor examples identify a 401(k) source plan. The five plan-family distribution/rollover candidates explicitly name Traditional IRA destinations, with acceptance still unresolved. Cash funding and cash settlement accounts are not made holdings of an insurance or retirement plan merely because a separate plan identity is required. Reference-bearing terms contain IDs; descriptive wording lives only in `*Description` annotations. Covered members, elected Medicare part, distribution character, contribution source, same-owner transfers, distinct transfer accounts and distinct rollover plans are checked against their corresponding roles/facts.

Symbolic accounts in the 31 cases without effects are **references only**, with no opening balances, valuation, funding availability or forecast transactions. For example, naming an FSA plan, payroll source and reimbursement destination does not establish uniform coverage, available reimbursement balance or legal eligibility. Missing-law terms remain explicit uncertainty. Ordinary 403(b), governmental 457(b), TSP and SIMPLE contribution examples represent employee salary deferrals; the ordinary SEP example represents an employer contribution with an employer-owned source. These examples do not assert that the employee examples cover employer match/nonelective contributions. Each family's boundary separately identifies unresolved contribution-limit, tax and distribution/rollover rules. In particular, the TSP account-label gap applies only to TSP, and the SEP case does not imply salary-reduction SEP support.

`test/d2/verifyEconomicContract.ts` explicitly admits exactly the existing 24 conditional projections and rejects adding/removing effects for those case IDs. It checks the supplied amounts against expected transaction identity, authored date, economic account, debit/credit side, amount and known facts. The independently stated closing balances and recognition totals in the JSON remain literal oracles reconciled separately. No test helper generates or replaces those expected values.

`contracts.test.ts` includes positive admission for every represented variant, required-field deletion checks for all 55 cases, unknown/mistyped source/destination/plan/person/employer references, contradictory ownership/plan links and source-character checks. Amount mutations leave expected postings untouched and must fail for the known benefit, withholding, transfer, draw, debt service, principal, interest and premium inputs. Authored posting/start/commencement/redemption dates and coverage mutations are checked, including failed-funding and excluded-date projections. A separately restated larger benefit quote with matching literal effects must pass. Changing an unresolved IRA aggregate basis or an excluded positive premium is intentionally permitted because neither changes the bounded asserted effects.

Reproducibility means the same catalog yields the same reconciliation and leaves inputs unchanged. Reversing account, identity and posting-leg inventories does not change validation or totals; transaction order is preserved. This is an algebraic fixture check, **not** proof that financial operations commute or that incidental order may decide economic precedence. No case executes household compilation, live scheduling, tax settlement or UI paths. Descriptive/legal annotations and the authority of supplied quotes still require independent review; machine-checkable shape, roles and accounting links do not establish their legal truth.

# Automated financial onboarding — coverage, CSV friction and vendor decision

**As-of 2026-10-11.** Vendor claims verified against primary vendor documentation; this is **planning**, not test-confirmed institution-specific production coverage, price quotation, procurement approval or proof of implementation. Related [Issue #108](https://github.com/bensmullen/personal-finance-app/issues/108) / [PR #109](https://github.com/bensmullen/personal-finance-app/pull/109). The synthetic eight-household exploration library is independent of the connectivity choice.

## Recommended product priority
**Direct read-only account aggregation should be the prospective default**, because getting multiple CSVs adds another institution-by-institution workflow. **One narrow, tested CSV fallback** can be valuable for failed connections or acquisition-lot history but should not displace an aggregation feasibility pilot. Do not build a universal importer or require CSV as the primary new-user journey before alpha uptake/effort evidence. The existing O1/PFA-ONB-003 requirement for an efficient assisted *bulk/file* path can be met through targeted imports/extraction, and should be revisited transparently if users choose direct links instead; live Plaid is **not** a mandatory R11 prerequisite. No new vendor contract or production external-data integration is authorized by this research.

## What the user actually does for a CSV or statement upload

1. In PFA choose **Import from file** > source (e.g. Schwab positions, Fidelity activity, checking transactions, mortgage statement). Show the supported file kind, required fields and a very short provider-specific guide; **the user does not compose a spreadsheet**.
2. Open the institution's existing **web portal**, authenticate/MFA, go to transaction history, holdings/positions, tax-lot details or statement/document center, and pick account + period. Select its **Export/Download** facility and save machine-generated CSV/Excel file, or download an already-issued PDF statement. Exact menu positions and availability differ by account/provider; do not pretend one universal instruction.
   - Verified Schwab *StreetSmart Edge* Positions screen: Actions or right-click > Export Positions **with Lots** > CSV or another format; ordinary Export Positions is **without lots**. Source: https://help.streetsmart.schwab.com/edge/1.72/content/Positions.htm . This is documented for StreetSmart Edge and does **not** establish identical UI in every current Schwab platform.
   - Fidelity shows processed **Activity & Orders > History** in Portfolio Summary and downloadable statements (up to 10 years published); *Fidelity Active Trader* history allows right-click Export after all rows load. Sources: https://www.fidelity.com/customer-service/account ; https://www.fidelity.com/products/atbt/help/ActiveTraderTools_History_Help.html . File type, range and availability are interface-specific.
   - Some banks expose downloads of checking/card activity, while other portals require monthly PDF statements. **Do not assert a particular consumer bank offers arbitrary CSV or complete history** without testing it. If PDF only, it is already generated by the lender/bank, but parsing a statement is harder than tabular import.
   - US residential mortgage servicers generally must issue periodic statements (subject to legal exceptions) containing principal balance, rate and principal/interest/escrow payment breakdown. They are **not automatically CSV exports**. Source: https://www.consumerfinance.gov/consumer-tools/mortgages/your-mortgage-servicer-must-comply-with-federal-rules/ ; https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/on-a-mortgage-whats-the-difference-between-my-principal-and-interest-payment-and-my-total-monthly-payment-en-1941/
3. Return to PFA and upload file(s). App detects *supported* format, previews period/account/rows, identifies balances/positions/lots/transactions/payments, and labels missing basis/interest/escrow/ownership fields rather than filling with guesses. For example a lot export might contain 300 acquisition rows with date, fund, shares, basis, account but no salary, current card bill or mortgage.
4. Review proposed account/lot imports, duplicates, historical overlap, user-owned transfers and classifications; explicitly accept. Normalized candidates route into existing source-of-funds & canonical-authoritative-financial rules; **no silent ledger mutation**.
5. App shows imported fact coverage/age, unresolved questions, a useful initial supported forecast, and options to separately link live accounts for refresh. A file upload is only a snapshot; future activity needs user re-uploads or a connected provider.

**Important:** Several file types may be needed per provider: bank spending history, brokerage *current holdings*, separate brokerage *tax-lot/cost basis* export, transaction/trade history, and mortgage PDF. An ordinary activity CSV **will not necessarily reconstruct 20 years of remaining lots or current holdings**, and a bank deposit does **not** reveal paystub gross income, retirement deduction, taxes, or employer benefit details.

## Plausible effort and value — scenarios, NOT observed benchmarks

| Profile and specific user behavior | CSV path (illustrative minutes) | Direct-link path (illustrative minutes) | Why it saves effort / what remains |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| One checking institution, 12 months of purchases; portal export available | 4–10 to find export, download, upload, review | 2–5 to authorize and review | Imports hundreds of transactions without manual transcription; 10–30% categorization corrections may still be needed; not a measured rate |
| Brokerage with 200 holdings/lot rows and separate cost-basis export | 8–20 for 1–3 exports and review | 3–8 if provider returns positions/lots; missing lots still need export | Hundreds of quantities/dates/bases entered in bulk; **direct link doesn't guarantee lot history** |
| 5 institutions: 2 banks, 2 brokerages, 1 mortgage servicer | 25–75, several files/format guides | 10–25 if all accounts supported, including review | Link likely beats file workflow on total onboarding friction and ongoing refresh; quote/test required |
| Mortgage statement only as PDF | 4–10 download/upload/review | 2–5 if liabilities covers it | A single statement saves only a handful of fields, not enough alone to justify a general PDF parser |

Estimates are **reasoned time-budget hypotheses, not usability evidence or guaranteed savings**. Assumes user already knows logins, no MFA lockouts and formats supported. A failed connection, confused export screen or unsupported format can erase the saving. Manual transcription of 300 tax lots would plausibly take hours and invite mistakes, but the exact minutes saved and file adoption must be **measured with real participants**, not asserted from the row count. No universal CSV engineering investment is justified without verified upload demand and a short representative export exercise.

## Provider proof-of-value instead of speculative importer build
1. Pick 5–10 consenting alpha candidates' **institution names and account types only** (no real data outside approved privacy boundary); construct a high-value coverage matrix with optional pilot results from synthetic/approved sources.
2. Attempt two export tasks with a small set of testers: (a) bank 12-month transaction export and (b) brokerage holding-with-lots export; observe time, errors, imports abandoned and actual usefulness. Do not implement 20 institution formats.
3. Run Plaid Trial for 10 allowed Items and SnapTrade Build test with permitted accounts, measuring actual banking + investment + liabilities coverage, account/position/lot recency, provider-failure and reconnect behavior; data protected with existing approved controls.
4. Evaluate provider bids for 100/1,000 connected users with 2–5 Items each; separate unit costs for transactions, balances, liabilities, investment holdings/transactions/refresh and optional lot add-ons; include privacy/retention/support and confidence in cost basis.
5. Release high-confidence read-only import review -> user-confirmed snapshot; allow users to postpone complex basis/compensation/tax details, show forecast incompleteness. Preserve idempotency and origin, duplicates, mixed family ownership, double card payment and mortgage escrow guards.
6. Only promote CSV to a wider product investment if it demonstrably removes a frequently observed provider gap **and** first-time user workflow stays simpler than manual entry. Keep demo-household UAT independent from any account connection vendor.
